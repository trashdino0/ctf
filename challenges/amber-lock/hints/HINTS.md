# Amber Lock — hints

Twelve escalating hints, one per lock. Each section is collapsed — click to open
only the one you need.

<details>
<summary>Lock 1 — the three-horned local</summary>

The name is Greek for "three-horned face." It was named by O. C. Marsh in 1889 and
lived in the Hell Creek Formation. Think of the one ceratopsian everyone knows.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 2 — the 1861 feather</summary>

"Ancient wing." Hermann von Meyer named a single Solnhofen feather in 1861. The
genus is the classic transitional fossil from every textbook.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 3 — different lizard</summary>

Break the Greek: *allos* + *sauros*. Marsh, 1877, Morrison Formation. It is the
big predator you see in Jurassic Park's visitor-center skeleton — but not the
one in the movie's title.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 4 — the titan's address</summary>

Look at the species epithet: *huinculensis*. The formation is in Neuquén Province,
Argentina, and also produced *Mapusaurus*.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 5 — not a dinosaur</summary>

The park's aquatic headline act. Latin for the Meuse river (*Mosa*) plus
"-saurus." It is a marine squamate, not a dinosaur.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 6 — who signed the name</summary>

Barnum Brown found it, but the name was published in 1905 by the president of the
American Museum of Natural History. Three initials: H. F. O.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 7 — bumps on the ulna</summary>

The 2007 *Science* paper is titled "Feather ______ in the Dinosaur
*Velociraptor*." Also known as ulnar papillae.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 8 — the branch never left</summary>

Literally "hand robbers." This coelurosaurian clade contains dromaeosaurs,
troodontids, and birds. Birds sit inside it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 9 — display feather</summary>

*Epi-* + *-dex-* + *-pteryx*. The species name honours the Chinese palaeontologist
Hu Yaoming. It is a tiny scansoriopterygid from the Daohugou beds.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 10 — the exceptional bed</summary>

Northeastern China, Liaoning. The Early Cretaceous Yixian and Jiufotang
formations together make up this famous Lagerstätte. The word appears in the
challenge's flag theme.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 11 — a name on thin ice</summary>

Latin for "dubious name." The plural is *nomina dubia*. It is what happens when a
holotype has no unique diagnostic features.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lock 12 — the platypus dinosaur</summary>

Chile + *sauros*. The species is *diegosuarezi*, after Diego Suárez, who found the
fossils at age seven. Described in 2015.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Assembly — the fragments are scrambled</summary>

Each released fragment shows an `IDX` code. Ignore the order the tray gives you
and place fragment `IDX 01` into slot 01, `IDX 02` into slot 02, and so on. The
assembled string should read like a leetspeak phrase: "amber clade index."

</details>
